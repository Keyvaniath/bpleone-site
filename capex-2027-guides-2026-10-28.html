A Mixed Answer From the Buyers

Two of the three buyers who have reported committed to spending more in 2027 and the third would not say; what none of them settled is who pays for it.

Posted October 29, 2026Author Brandon LeonData as of company releases and calls as reported; Amazon reports Thursday and is added in a dated update

The verdict

No buyer stepped back. Meta, about a fifth of the four plans at the midpoint of its 2026 range, gave no 2027 direction at all: “we expect to continue investing aggressively,” with the budget still being built. Our own wording from October 19 requires restraint here: one company guiding lower needs its size and reasons examined before it means anything about the cycle.[15] That is neither a build pause nor a demand statement; it is a budget not yet set, and the disclosures do not allow a determination.

The other two pointed higher, so the demand case is intact on the majority of the spending and unproven on the rest. That is a narrower conclusion than either of the clean outcomes would have allowed, and it leaves the financing question exactly where October 19 left it.

1. What the buyers had already said

Everyone was waiting for the 2027 numbers. The buyers had already answered the question underneath them: each of the four raised its plan for the current year at least once in 2026, and that record is the baseline the new guides are judged against.

Alphabet opened the year at $175 to $185 billion and had reached $195 to $205 billion by July, having told shareholders in June that 2027 would “significantly increase” from there.[1][2][3] Amazon said about $200 billion in February and about $220 billion in July, with the chief executive attributing the increase to the cost of memory and adding that demand would exceed capacity “in 2027, too.”[11][13] Meta started at $115 to $135 billion, raised the range in April on component prices and has since narrowed it upward twice, to $135 to $145 billion last week.[7][8][9][10] Microsoft, on a June fiscal year, said in July that fiscal 2027 spending “will grow year-over-year” and put the figure at about $175 billion after moving new data-center leases off the capital-spending line.[6]

Add the four current-year plans and the figure is about $735 billion, against roughly $410 billion the same companies spent in their last completed year.[1][5][7][11] The definitions differ, and Microsoft’s fiscal year straddles two calendar years, so treat the sum as a measure of scale and nothing more precise. Even as scale, it says the buyers were committed to nearly twice last year’s total before anyone asked them about 2027.

So the live question was narrower than the headlines suggested: would the buyers commit to more in 2027 than the roughly $735 billion already planned, and how would they pay for it. The first half is the demand case the October 19 note said this week would settle. The second half is the financing question it said this week could not.

2. How the market has been grading the raises

Which of the two halves the market cares about is also already on the record. The July reports are the best guide, because all four companies raised or held their plans within nine days and the reactions split cleanly. Microsoft left its calendar-2026 plan unchanged, backed it with Azure growth of 43% and a contracted backlog, and rose 15.5% the next session. Amazon raised to about $220 billion with AWS accelerating to 37% growth and rose 15.3%. Alphabet raised to $195 to $205 billion in the same quarter it reported its first negative free cash flow and after raising $49.6 billion of equity and $56.2 billion of debt in six months, and fell 7.1%. Meta raised its range alongside free cash flow of under $1 billion and fell 8.0%.[4][5][12][14]

October has so far delivered only the sold half of that pattern. Meta narrowed its range upward again on October 21, reported negative free cash flow for the quarter, and fell 7.0% the next day. Lam Research, which sells the equipment this spending buys, guided its December quarter to $8.6 billion against the $8.1 billion we had registered as the proof level, and fell 11.8%.[10][14][17] Good news on the spending line has been sold for two weeks. The market is grading two other things: whether the spending is paid for from operations, and whether the revenue it is meant to produce is showing up.

That is why each of this week’s reports carries a proof test we set before it, each at a 55% prior: Azure growth above the 45% Microsoft guided, Google Cloud backlog above $560 billion, and AWS growth above the 37% of the second quarter.[16] A raised 2027 plan that arrives with its proof test passed is the July Microsoft and Amazon pattern. A raised plan with the proof missed, or with new external financing, is the Alphabet and Meta pattern, and on the evidence of the last three months the shares will say so by the next close.

One accounting point decides how to read Microsoft’s number. The ~$175 billion for fiscal 2027 is after a change that moves new data-center leases from finance to operating treatment; finance leases count as capital spending and operating leases do not, and the company said the shift lowered the figure it would otherwise have given.[6] A reported number that comes in under what the Street carried may therefore be an accounting effect, with the build unchanged. The operating-lease commitments in the quarterly filing are where the difference shows, and we read that line before calling anything a cut.

Meta is the buyer that did not point higher, and it did not point lower either. Its October 21 release gave no 2027 figure and no direction, only “we expect to continue investing aggressively to support both our core business and emerging AI initiatives,” with the 2027 budget still in development. At the midpoint of its 2026 plan it is about 19% of the four. Nothing in the disclosures allows a cycle reading from it either way.

The demand case stands on the buyers who pointed higher and is unproven on the one who did not. The financing question is unchanged by either. Both proof tests at the buyers who pointed higher passed: Azure grew 48% against the 45% line and Google Cloud’s backlog reached $561 billion against the $560 billion line, by one billion.

What it means: Alphabet said it will spend significantly more next year and Microsoft said its spending accelerates into the first half of 2027; Meta would only say it keeps investing aggressively. The same calls showed the cost: Alphabet’s free cash flow was negative $9 billion for the quarter and Microsoft said it will tap outside funding when it is attractive.

5. What would make us reconsider

  1. The proof tests, graded five sessions after each report. Azure above 45%, Cloud backlog above $560 billion, AWS above 37%, each registered at a 55% prior. Azure 48%, passed; Google Cloud backlog $561 billion, passed by one billion; AWS reports Thursday. The five-session grades follow on November 4.
  2. A new test from what the guides revealed. The aggregate rose, so the next question is funding. The read changes from “demand intact, financing open” to “demand bought with leverage” if Alphabet’s fourth-quarter free cash flow is negative again alongside new issuance in the 10-Q, or if Microsoft’s “additional sources of funding” becomes a raise of $20 billion or more before its fiscal second quarter closes. If neither happens, the demand case stands as written.
  3. CoreWeave credit, standing. A five-year default-swap spread back below 800 basis points says the credit scare is healing. At or above 1,140, the October high, says it is deepening.
  4. The ten-year, standing. A Friday close at or above 5.31%, October’s cycle high, keeps the pressure on long-duration equity; sustained below it eases one source of it.
  5. The semiconductor index. A weekly close above the October 16 close, after a capital-spending beat the index finishes up on, ends the no-bottom-call rule. Until then the rule stands.

Amazon, added November 2

Added November 2, 2026, 3:30 PM ET. Amazon reported on October 29. The 2026 plan held: “We continue to expect 2026 cash capital expenditures to be approximately $220 billion.” For 2027 the release gives the demand side, not the spending side: “projected revenue growth rates of over 40% both in 2027 and in 2028” for AWS, which grew 39% in the quarter against the 37% proof test. On the call Andy Jassy said 2027 spending would be meaningfully higher, as reported by several outlets; Amazon had not published a transcript when this was written, so the words are secondhand and no figure was given. The funding line is the starkest of the four: trailing free cash flow was an outflow of $42.4 billion at September 30, against $7.6 billion at June 30, with $77.3 billion of purchases of property and equipment in the quarter.[19]

Amazon does not change the branch. Three of the four buyers now point higher for 2027, Amazon with the weakest free cash flow of the group, and Meta remains the one that gave no direction. The read above stands as written: the demand case on three, unproven on one, and the financing question wider, not narrower.
