How this works

Sources & Uses. Entry EV = entry multiple × LTM EBITDA. New debt = leverage × EBITDA. Sponsor equity plugs the gap (plus 2% fees). Existing debt assumed refinanced (clean capital structure at close).

Debt schedule. Each year: 1% mandatory amort + 100% × CFADS sweep (after capex, NWC, taxes, interest) — capped so debt can't go below zero. Net Debt / EBITDA and Interest Coverage tracked through the hold; covenant breach (Lev > 7× or Cov < 1.5×) auto-flags.

Sponsor IRR. Bisection on the cash-flow stream: −sponsor equity at t=0, exit equity (= exit EV − exit debt + retained cash) at hold-end. MOIC = total cash returned ÷ equity in.

Returns attribution. Decomposes the sponsor's value creation into three drivers: EBITDA growth = (exit EBITDA − entry EBITDA) × entry multiple; multiple expansion = exit EBITDA × (exit mult − entry mult); debt paydown + retained cash. If multiple expansion dominates, the deal is a re-rating bet — fragile. If EBITDA growth dominates, it's an operational thesis.

Reverse-LBO. Solves the inverse: given the exit multiple and leverage you set, what's the maximum entry multiple at which a sponsor still hits target IRR? Convert to an implied take-private price. Useful framework even outside actual buyout situations — tells you where private capital would step in.

Sensitivity. Re-runs the full LBO across an entry × exit multiple grid. Green cells = above 20% IRR (PE-financeable). Yellow = 15–20%. Red = below 15% or negative.
