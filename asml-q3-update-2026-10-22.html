Coverage update · Semicap · ASML Q3 2026

The Shortage Runs Through 2028

ASML cleared the top of its margin guidance, raised the year a third time and said it cannot build enough machines for 2027 or 2028. Estimates rose 9%. The stock fell 14% from the day we initiated and closed tonight on the floor of the add range we published in July. This note re-sets the target, says why, and scores the call.

Author Brandon Leon Posted Thursday, October 22, 2026 · revised after the close the same day (price, consensus and the target; see the revision note at the end) Data Q3 results of October 14; price, consensus and FX as of the October 22 close Rating Accumulate, unchanged since the July 15 initiation · target about $2,075, from about $2,000

On October 14 ASML reported third-quarter sales of €11.66 billion, toward the high end of its €11 to 12 billion guidance, at a gross margin of 57.5%, above the top of the 55 to 57% it had promised. It raised the year to €44 to 45 billion, the third raise of 2026, and guided the December quarter to €14.2 to 15.2 billion, the largest in its history. Then it said the thing that matters more than the numbers: planned supply will not fully meet customer demand in 2027 or 2028, and some customers are asking for 2029 and 2030 slots.1

The stock closed at $1,560.54 tonight, down 6.9% on the day and 14% below the $1,815 of July 15, when we initiated. The semicap ETF is down 11% over the same stretch and the S&P 500 is up 1%.3 The July note said we would add on a semiconductor-wide pullback into $1,550 to $1,650 and would not chase. The stock entered that range on October 15 and tonight it sits on the bottom of it. The business did what we underwrote and the price did the opposite, and this note has to hold both facts at once.

1. What the quarter said

Start with the margin, because it was supposed to be the problem. The July guide for this quarter was 55 to 57% and the worry was mix, more DUV and more China in the shipments. The print came in at 57.5%, up from 54.0% in June, and the lift came from installed-base management, the service and upgrade revenue on machines already in the field, which reached €3.3 billion, about €400 million more than ASML expected. That is the most predictable revenue the company has, and it is growing into a fleet that keeps getting larger.

The year moved again, to €44 to 45 billion at a margin of about 56%. ASML sold 121 systems in the quarter, 113 of them new, with EUV at €4.1 billion of system sales including one High-NA tool. It bought back about €4.1 billion of stock, nearly four times the pace of the prior quarter, and the dividend is €1.88 a share, payable November 4.1

The supply statement changes the shape of the next two years. Capacity for low-NA EUV and DUV immersion rises 30% in 2027, more than that is being studied for 2028, and demand is still expected to run ahead of what can be built through at least 2028. On the call the finance chief said price increases should begin to lift revenue and gross margin during 2027, with the full effect in 2028, and declined to put a number on the 2027 margin until January.2 For the only company that makes these machines, that is a pricing statement wearing a capacity statement's clothes.

One caution on the profit line. Net income of €4.96 billion included roughly €500 million from marking up ASML's stake in Mistral AI. That is a valuation gain, not cash from selling machines, and we set it aside when we judge the quarter.2

2. The shares went the other way

The stock fell 3.9% on the day of the print and has fallen most days since. Nothing in the release explains that, so the explanation has to come from outside it. From the initiation to mid-October the 10-year yield rose from 4.55% to 5.22%, and a company whose value sits in cash flows years away gets repriced by that move more than most. The whole complex was repriced with it, which is why the semicap ETF is down 11% over the window too.

Tonight does not fit that story and we should say so rather than stretch it. The 10-year fell 20 basis points on October 22 and the stock fell 6.9% anyway, with the semicap ETF down 6%. That is a day when the sector is sold as a block whatever the bond market does, and it is the kind of day the July note had in mind when it tied the add range to a semiconductor-wide pullback rather than to ASML news.

3. Estimates up, multiple down

This is the part of the record that holds. When we re-cut the target on August 18 the Street expected 2027 earnings of €51.42 a share. After the print the figure is €55.93, up 8.8%, and the 2026 number moved the same way, to €41.21 from €38.03.4 The price moved against that. In August the stock paid 30.1 times the 2027 estimate. Tonight, at $1,560.54 and with the euro at 1.12 instead of 1.16, it pays 24.9 times a higher one. The multiple is down 17% in nine weeks while the earnings under it rose 9%. In August we wrote that the growth was not the risk and the multiple was. That is what happened, faster and further than the bear case we drew.

4. The tree, re-set, and the target

The July target of about $2,000 was held through the August re-cut and the September record review on the rule that a target does not move for noise. The print was not noise, so the tree is re-set here from its stated inputs, with every change listed.

The weights stay where August put them: 30% bull, 45% base, 25% bear. The earnings base is the new 2027 consensus, €55.93, converted at tonight's 1.1221, which is $62.76 a share. The bull case keeps 38 to 40 times, where the stock traded at its June record, and lands at $2,385 to $2,510. The base case keeps the multiple non-monopoly peers carried when we last measured them in August, 33 to 36 times, and lands at $2,070 to $2,260. The bear case had to change. In August it assumed a rates haircut to 27 or 28 times; the stock now trades below that, so the bear is re-anchored at 22 to 25 times, $1,380 to $1,570, a range whose floor is the stock's low of the last six months.

Weighted, that is about $2,075. The consensus raise lifts every branch by about 9%, the stronger dollar takes a third of that back, and the lower bear takes a little more. The result is 3.7% above the published figure, outside the 3% band we hold targets inside, so the target moves to about $2,075, 33% above tonight's close.

Scoring the July call. From $1,815 the stock is down 14% against a flat market. The call was right about the company: three guide raises, a margin four and a half points higher, estimates up 9% and a supply statement stronger than the one we hoped for. It was wrong about the price, for the reason the August note named as the risk. We also wrote an add range in July and did not use it. The single share held at the initiation was sold before the September review, so there is no position tonight, and a purchase in the range would be a re-entry at the level we named three months ago. Our trading rule bars an order in a covered name for five days after a note on it publishes, and because this revision changes the target it restarts that clock. The next note will report whether an order was placed and at what level, and if nothing was done it will say that.

The range, restated

The add range stands as published in July: $1,550 to $1,650, on a semiconductor-wide pullback, without chasing. Tonight's close is $1,560.54. The range was written with the 10-year at 4.5% and the yield is 4.95% now, so a reader is entitled to ask whether it belongs to a regime that no longer exists. Our answer is that the range was set against the stock's own July low, that low has held twice, and the bear case in the new tree starts where the range ends.

What makes this note wrong

A company that says it cannot build enough machines for two years has every reason to say so, and the hard number that would confirm it, bookings, is one ASML stopped disclosing. The re-set tree leans on a 2027 estimate that rose 9% in nine weeks and could be cut in January if the first 2027 framing disappoints. The base case assumes a re-rating toward peer multiples we have not re-measured since August. And the author held this name at the initiation, does not hold it tonight, and may buy it once the window closes, so read a reaffirmed positive rating with that in mind.

What it means: The company did better than it promised and told investors it cannot build fast enough for two more years. Analysts raised their earnings numbers. The shares fell anyway, first because higher interest rates lower what people will pay today for profits that arrive years out, and this week because the whole chip sector was sold together. The stock now sits at the bottom of the range where we said in July we would buy. We are keeping the rating, raising the target a little because the estimates rose, showing exactly how the number was built, and we will report what we actually do once our own trading rule allows it.
