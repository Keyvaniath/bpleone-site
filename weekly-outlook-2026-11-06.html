Weekly outlook · recap · the week to November 6, 2026

The Market Did Not Buy the Beat

The market did not buy AMD’s beat, as the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) closed the week at 463.96, below the 546.40 line set in our Wednesday outlook, −11.2% on the week and −18.3% since the first 2027 spending guides, while the S&P 500 fell 2.7%. October payrolls rose 165,000 versus the 59,000 expected by economists polled by Dow Jones, the Kalshi price of a December rate increase stood at 77.5% late Friday after the report, and Vistra initiated 2027 adjusted EBITDA guidance with an $8.1B midpoint. Our first test resolved in the direction predicted by our financing reading, that investors are repricing the companies most dependent on outside money to fund the AI buildout, while the third was inconclusive. The December test remains pending until Kalshi’s daily close is available.

Author Brandon Leon Posted Friday, November 6, 2026, after the close Data Prices as of Friday’s close (Yahoo Finance); the December-increase price as of 4:33 p.m. ET Friday (Kalshi via DefiRate); the October employment report and Vistra’s release as published Coverage The week in five sessions · the scorecard · what Friday added · where the bill sits · what we carry forward

We Read the Week as a Repricing of the Sellers of AI Capacity

We expected the market to keep selling the suppliers of AI capacity after AMD’s report, and it did. SMH closed at 463.96, 15.1% below the 546.40 line set in our Wednesday outlook, after an 11.2% fall on Thursday that was its worst session since March 2020 and a 3.6% rebound on Friday (Chart 1).1,2 Across the week the ten-year ended at 4.86% and the high-yield fund moved −0.3%, while the Kalshi price of a December rate increase went from 80.5% to 64.5% and back to 77.5%, so the losses landed far harder on chip shares than on the broad market, the bond market or credit. We read that as a repricing inside the sector with financing exposure an important part of it, although returns alone cannot isolate how large a part, and the Democratic sweep added a question on Thursday about whether policy changes could slow approvals for the buildout.

1. The week in five sessions

The Market Asked a Different Question Each Day and Graded the Suppliers on Every One

  • Monday: Oracle said it would sell $10B of stock and $10B of bonds for its AI buildout and Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at about $115B of enterprise value, so the week opened with the suppliers raising money and the chip ETF closing at 508.31.3
  • Tuesday: AMD reported a third-quarter non-GAAP gross margin of 56%, meeting the line we had registered, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to approximately $17B, above the $16.14B consensus our October 26 coverage named as the number to beat, and overnight projections showed Democrats winning control of both chambers of Congress.3
  • Wednesday: The Dow rose more than 1,500 points in its best session since April 2025 while the chip ETF fell 2.7%. AMD rose 1.0% in its first session after reporting, the first of the five prints we tested this season that was not sold on the day, and our weekly, dated that Wednesday, carried its tests into the rest of the week.2
  • Thursday: All three averages had their worst day since April 2025 and the chip ETF lost 11.2%, its worst session since March 2020, as the sweep was read as a threat to the pace of the data-center buildout and AMD gave back 17.3%, with the selling spreading well beyond technology.2
  • Friday: Payrolls rose 165,000 versus the 59,000 expected by economists polled by Dow Jones, and the chip ETF recovered 3.6% with AMD up 11.0%, while the Dow slipped 0.5%.4,5

For the week the S&P 500 fell 2.7%, the Nasdaq Composite fell 3.4%, the Dow fell 0.9% and the semiconductor ETF fell 11.2%, and the VIX ended at 25.6 after touching 28 on Thursday. The losses were disproportionately concentrated in semiconductors, although Thursday’s selloff spread across the broad market (Chart 1).1

2. The scorecard

The Market Did Not Buy the Beat

Our first test said that, with AMD’s guide at or above $16.14B, a semiconductor ETF close on Friday above 546.40 would mean the market had bought a beat and our no-bottom rule would lift. A close at or below it would mean the de-rating had moved past the numbers and the financing reading held (Chart 2).6 SMH closed at 463.96, 15.1% under the line, so under its original criteria the test left our financing reading intact, and the rule from October 23, no bottom call until the market buys a beat, stands into its final reading on November 20.

December Recovered on the Labor Data

Our second test said a Kalshi price of a December increase at or above 69.5% at Friday’s close would mean the committee’s path survived the labor data, below it that the data had started to move December, and below 50% the regime change we named after the October 28 hold.7 The price fell to 64.5% at Thursday’s close on the selloff, a day before the October employment report, and stood at 77.5% at 4:33 p.m. ET Friday after the report, so the reading is provisionally consistent with the committee’s December path remaining intact, while the test itself grades on Kalshi’s daily close in the DefiRate series, which we log in our ledger when it posts (Chart 3).

Friday Left Thursday’s Selloff Inconclusive

The third line, which we registered in our ledger on Thursday evening before Friday’s open, said a semiconductor ETF close above 470.00 would read Thursday as positioning and a close at or below 447.73, Thursday’s close, as a selloff with legs, with any close between those thresholds deemed inconclusive.6 SMH closed at 463.96, inside the inconclusive range our line specified, after taking back 29% of Thursday’s loss, so under its original criteria the line supports neither a positioning-only interpretation nor a sustained-selloff interpretation. The standing indicators give a mixed reading, as the ten-year at 4.86% sits well below the 5.31% that puts the pressure back while Lumentum ended at a 15.2% discount to the implied value of the 4.5 Marvell shares offered per share, past the 15% at which our card says the market doubts the close.1

3. What Friday added

A Stronger-Than-Expected Payroll Report Was Followed by Higher Odds of a December Increase

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that payrolls rose 165,000 in October versus the 59,000 expected by economists polled by Dow Jones, with August and September revised up by a combined 84,000, the unemployment rate at 4.1% and average hourly earnings up 0.4% on the month and 3.1% on the year.4 Breadth: professional and business services added 70,000, health care 42,000, transportation and warehousing 25,000 and manufacturing 19,000, gains across several industries that a committee that raised rates in September can read as room to raise again. The Kalshi price of a December increase rose from 64.5% to 77.5%, 13 percentage points, while the ten-year ended at 4.86%. The chips: the semiconductor ETF rose 3.6% on the same day, which does not fit a simple account in which higher odds of an increase drive chip shares down.7

Vistra’s 2027 Guidance Midpoint Matched the Number Our October Note Set

Vistra reported third-quarter Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA of $2,378M against $1,581M a year earlier and narrowed its 2026 guidance to $7.1B to $7.5B. It initiated 2027 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $7.7B to $8.5B, a midpoint of $8.1B that matches the number our October 3 note set, with $4.3B to $5.1B of adjusted free cash flow before growth and an additional $1.5B share-repurchase authorization.8 Our note asked for $8.1B including Cogentrix and Meta and the release does not say what the range includes, so the grade against that wording belongs to our Vistra coverage, which will read the call and the filings. The release also announced a 20-year, 207 MW power purchase agreement at its Odessa plant in Texas and said its 405 MW Pulaski solar facility in Illinois had commenced operations under a power purchase agreement with Microsoft, and the shares rose 10.6% on the day.

4. Where the bill sits

Most Hyperscale Buyers Held Up While the Suppliers and Financing-Exposed Names Fell

Measured from the October 28 close, the night the first 2027 guides arrived, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta are between −0.4% and +1.8%, with Alphabet the exception at −12.3%. The suppliers are down 10% to 30%, the five companies in our sample that raised money or paid in shares 20% to 44%, the two listed renters of capacity 25% to 29% and the two data-center landlords in our sample −3.1% to +0.5% (Chart 4).1 That ordering is what the financing reading predicts, with the companies that need outside money to build down the most and the companies whose cash pays for the buildout down the least, although a ranking of returns cannot by itself separate financing from valuation. Bank of America’s Wamsi Mohan made the valuation side of that point on Friday, writing that the sell-off in AI-exposed hardware “has come from lower valuation multiples, not lower earnings estimates.”5

Friday’s Rebound Partly Reversed Thursday

Friday’s rebound sorted by Thursday’s damage, with a correlation of −0.62 between the two days’ moves across 22 names, which is consistent with a partial reversal of Thursday’s selling but cannot tell positioning apart from a change in fundamentals or in appetite for risk (Chart 5). The chip ETF took back 29% of Thursday’s loss, short of the 470.00 our third line set, so on our own framework Thursday reads neither as positioning nor as a selloff with legs. The renters’ side of the bill also arrived in writing, as Iren reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $228M against $137M in the prior quarter and said more than $4B of annual recurring revenue “has been delayed until at least January 2027,” which moves part of a renter’s revenue into next year.5

5. What we carry forward

Nvidia’s Report Is the Next Beat the Market Can Buy or Sell

We carry the financing reading into the second half of the month, with the no-bottom rule standing until the market buys a beat and its final reading on November 20. We see Nvidia’s report later this month as the next earnings release that could lift the no-bottom rule, because its quarter speaks to demand directly and, through its receivables and its investments in customers, to who is paying for the buildout. The lines below are standing reading guides carried from earlier notes and not new tests, and the next weekly sets the next tests before the events they grade.

What would change our view — standing lines, not new tests
  1. The no-bottom rule (final reading November 20): it lifts only when the market buys a beat, and a chip ETF weekly close above 546.40 remains the line that says it has.
  2. December into the December 9 meeting: the Kalshi price at or above 69.5% keeps the committee’s path intact; below 50% is the regime change we named on October 28.
  3. The ten-year: a Friday close at or above 5.31% puts the pressure back, and above 5.10% says the supply is biting.
  4. The financing names: Lumentum’s discount to the implied value of 4.5 Marvell shares wider than 15% says the market doubts the close and under 8% says it believes it; CoreWeave’s five-year credit default swap spread below 800 basis points heals and at or above 1,140 deepens.
What makes this note wrong

A week that held a midterm sweep, a supplier equity wave, a stronger-than-expected payroll report and the largest single-day moves in chip shares since 2020 cannot be assigned to one cause, and the skeptic will say we read our October thesis into all of it. The financing reading rests on five companies that each had their own reason to raise money and on a ranking of returns that fits the reading without proving it, while the policy reading rests on television framing and a governor-elect’s comments and not on a bill or a rule. The author holds several of the names discussed, which is a reason to check this reading harder and not a reason to trust it.

What it means: AMD’s good report did not stop the selling in chip shares, Thursday’s drop in the chip ETF was the largest since 2020 and spread across the market, and a strong jobs report on Friday was followed by a partial rebound even as the odds of a December rate increase rose. Three of the four big cloud buyers are roughly where they were before their spending guides, while most of the companies that sell them chips and capacity are down by double digits.

6. Something to think about

The Odds of an Increase and the Chips Moved Together

On Thursday the Kalshi price of a December increase fell 16 percentage points while the chip ETF fell 11.2%. On Friday the price rose 13 percentage points after the payroll report while the chip ETF rose 3.6%, so over these two sessions the odds of tighter money and the chip shares moved in the same direction.

That pattern does not fit a simple account in which higher odds of an increase drive chip shares down. The more plausible reading is that the December price itself responded to Thursday’s selloff and to Friday’s data, although two sessions cannot isolate financing or policy as the cause that moved the chips instead. It is also the reason we grade on lines written before the events and not on the explanation of the day, because the explanation changed four times in five sessions while the closes kept their own record.

Which leaves the question for the rest of the month: if the odds of tighter money and the price of the chips keep moving together, what is the market telling us it fears more than the Fed?
